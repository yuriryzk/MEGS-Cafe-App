Menu

  • Three menu groups, then that group's boards. The 16-tab strip, and the 116 rows that listed 72 items, are gone.
  • The whole row is one button. The “+” that opened the product page anyway is removed, and so is the button nested inside a button.
  • Every row shows its price range (“₱130 to ₱140”), so a ₱130 row never becomes ₱140 without warning.
  • Best Seller is a small pale tag on its own line, so the name and description keep their full width and every row stays one height.

Choose

  • The photo sits on a white plate at full height, not in a dark box that was mostly empty.
  • Sizes list the board's own prices (₱130 · ₱140 · ₱140) instead of “+₱10” differences, with every size visible at once, the fix a Starbucks app study arrived at.
  • Quantity and the live total share one bar with 44px buttons, following Baymard's quantity-selector research.
  • No star ratings or reviews: the café has none to show. The honest signal is its own Best Seller mark.

Pay

  • Pickup and its ready time lead, as in Sweetgreen's ordering flow, with the lit lamp from the app's tracker.
  • Every line has its photo, its options and a 44px stepper. Totals are computed from the lines.
  • One primary action carries the total, so the price is never off screen.

References: Starbucks app usability test · Baymard, quantity selector design · The UX of Sweetgreen
